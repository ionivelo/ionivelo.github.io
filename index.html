Same switchboard

Same switchboard. A second way in.

Nobody in the house has to learn anything. The switch on the wall is still the fastest way to turn on a light. The app is just there for when you're not standing next to it.

On the wall

The switch didn't move, and it doesn't need to. A relay behind a mechanical toggle can't throw the toggle. Whatever position it's sitting in, the next flip toggles.

In the app
Straight to the device. About 180 ms, no cloud hop.

Flip a switch on the plate, or tap a tile in the app. Both work, and both stay in sync. Watch the difference: a wall flip is confirmed the moment it happens, while a tap from the app stays an outline until the device itself answers. The app never fills a tile it hasn't heard back about.

What still works

So what actually happens when the internet goes?

Flip the switches. We'll show you exactly what still works, and exactly what doesn't. Both matter.

You are

Your Wi-Fi is still your Wi-Fi.

The broadband being down doesn't take your router down. The app talks to the device directly, at full speed, exactly like it does every other day.

The honest catch: the app can't cold-start with the internet down. If it was already open, you're fine. If you'd force-closed it, you're back to the wall switch, which is exactly where everyone else already is.

Fan regulator

A fan regulator. Not a fan on/off.

Three, four or five real speed steps. Whatever your fan is wired for, the device tells the app. And it remembers the speed separately from on/off, so off-then-on comes back at speed 3, not speed 1.

Device reports

SPEED 3 / 5

Speed is remembered separately from on/off. Turn the fan off and on, or cut the power, and it comes back at speed 3.

Drag the dial, tap a detent, or focus it and use the arrow keys. It is a real slider underneath. The fan speed relays are wired into the fan's live wire and select the motor capacitor, which is why the speed survives a power cut: it is written to the device's own storage, not to a server.

No small print

The things we won't pretend about.

Away from home, it's a check-in, not a push.
The device asks for new instructions every 15 seconds. Worst case, about twenty seconds from tap to click. At home on your Wi-Fi it's under a second.
The app can't cold-start with the internet down.
If it's already open, local control keeps working. If you'd force-closed it, you're back to the wall switch, which is exactly where you started, and it works.
We install it. You don't.
Your Wi-Fi is written into the unit and it's fitted behind your board by our team. No QR codes, no pairing screen, no “device not found”.
Four channels per unit.
Three lights and a fan, or four lights. Bigger board, second unit.
Toggle switches only.
The old-style flip switches. Push-button switches double-toggle, so we swap them during the install.
Fan speed and schedules need the right hardware.
Both are ESP32-only, and the fan needs the speed relays wired to your regulator. We confirm what your fan supports during the survey.
No app store listing yet.
We put the app on your phone when we install, and update it for you until it's listed.

We'd rather you read this now than find it out later. If any of it is a dealbreaker, tell us on the call and we'll say so honestly rather than sell you a box.
